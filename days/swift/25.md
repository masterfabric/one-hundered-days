# Day 25: SwiftUI Basics - Extracting Reusable Views

## 📝 Today's Tasks

1.  **Extract a Subview:** Pull a chunk of `body` into its own `View` struct.
2.  **Pass Data In:** Give the subview `let` properties for its inputs.
3.  **Keep Views Small:** Refactor a large body into focused, named components.
4.  **Preview Components in Isolation:** Add a `#Preview` for the extracted view alone.
5.  **Review & Practice:** Rebuild a screen from Days 21–24 as a composition of extracted views, each with its own `#Preview`.

## 📖 Task Explanations

Take a screen you've already built and find a self-contained piece of its `body` — a row, a header, a card. Move it into its own `struct` conforming to `View`. Xcode can do the mechanical part for you: put the cursor on the outermost view of that chunk and use *Extract Subview* from the refactor menu, then rename it to something meaningful.

An extracted view needs its data, and the simplest way to supply it is plain `let` properties set from the call site. Keep those inputs as narrow as possible: a row that only renders a title and a date should take a title and a date, not the whole model object. Narrow inputs are what make a component reusable on the next screen instead of welded to this one.

Repeat until each view does one recognisable thing. A monolithic `body` is hard to read, slow to type-check, and forces SwiftUI to re-evaluate more than it needs to; a set of small named views is easier to reason about and lets SwiftUI update only the parts whose data actually changed. Naming matters here too — `OrderSummaryRow` tells you far more at a glance than a nested pile of stacks.

Finally, add a `#Preview` for the extracted view on its own, passing sample values directly. Previewing a component in isolation is much faster than driving the whole screen into the right state, and it pushes you towards views that don't secretly depend on their surroundings. This discipline of composing screens from many small, independently previewable views is one of the biggest differences between beginner and professional SwiftUI code.

Close the SwiftUI basics block by rebuilding one screen from Days 21–24 — a stack of modifiers, a `#Preview`, the `View` protocol in action — as named subviews rather than one `body`. Keep each extracted view's inputs narrow, preview it alone, then compose the screen from those parts. If you cannot rebuild it without peeking, that is the page to re-read before layout work starts on Day 26.

## 💡 What You'll Gain

By the end of today, you will decompose large views into small, reusable components, improving readability, reuse, and rendering performance from day one, and you will have reviewed Days 21–24 by recomposing a screen from those parts.

## 📚 Dictionary for Developers

*   **Subview:** A view extracted into its own struct for reuse and clarity.
*   **Composition:** Building complex UI from many small views.
*   **Component:** A reusable, self-contained view with clear inputs.
*   **View Identity:** How SwiftUI tracks a view across updates to decide what to redraw.
*   **Extract Subview:** Xcode's refactor action that moves a chunk of `body` into a new view.
*   **`#Preview`:** The macro declaring a preview of a view with sample data.
*   **Section Review:** Rebuilding a block's skills from memory to find gaps before the next section.
