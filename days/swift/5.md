# Day 5: Swift Fundamentals - Collections & Closures

## 📝 Today's Tasks

1.  **Use `Array`, `Set`, `Dictionary`:** Create and mutate each; know when order, uniqueness, or key-lookup matters.
2.  **Iterate and Transform:** Apply `map`, `filter`, and `reduce` to a collection.
3.  **Write a Closure:** Store a closure in a variable and call it; understand trailing-closure syntax.
4.  **Capture Values:** Observe how a closure captures variables from its surrounding scope.

## 📖 Task Explanations

Collections and closures are the workhorses of everyday Swift. `Array` is an ordered list, `Set` an unordered unique collection, and `Dictionary` a key-value map — choosing the right one encodes your intent. Higher-order methods like `map`, `filter`, and `reduce` let you transform data declaratively instead of writing manual loops. Closures are self-contained blocks of functionality that can be passed around like values and can capture state from where they were created. They power SwiftUI's callbacks, async APIs, and functional pipelines, so getting comfortable with trailing-closure syntax now pays off everywhere later.

## 💡 What You'll Gain

You will manipulate data with the three core collections and write closures — the foundation of functional-style Swift and every SwiftUI callback you will write.

## 📚 Dictionary for Developers

*   **Array/Set/Dictionary:** Ordered list, unique unordered set, and key-value map collection types.
*   **Higher-Order Function:** A function that takes or returns another function, like `map` or `filter`.
*   **Closure:** A self-contained block of code that can be stored, passed, and can capture context.
*   **Capture:** A closure holding onto variables from its surrounding scope.
