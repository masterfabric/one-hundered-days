# Day 77: UI Testing & Accessibility - VoiceOver & Semantics

## 📝 Today's Tasks

1.  **Add Accessibility Labels:** Give views clear `.accessibilityLabel` text.
2.  **Set Traits & Values:** Use `.accessibilityAddTraits` and `.accessibilityValue`.
3.  **Group Elements:** Combine related views with `.accessibilityElement(children:)`.
4.  **Test with VoiceOver:** Navigate your app using VoiceOver.

## 📖 Task Explanations

Accessibility is both an ethical obligation and a mark of quality. VoiceOver reads your interface aloud for users who can't see it, and it relies on the semantic information you provide: clear labels, correct traits (button, header, selected), and values (a slider's current setting). Grouping related elements makes navigation efficient. SwiftUI provides good defaults, but custom views need explicit accessibility annotations. Actually turning on VoiceOver and navigating your own app is eye-opening — it reveals gaps no visual review would catch, and fixing them helps everyone.

## 💡 What You'll Gain

You will make your app usable with VoiceOver by providing correct semantic labels, traits, and values, and by testing it hands-on.

## 📚 Dictionary for Developers

*   **VoiceOver:** Apple's screen reader for non-visual navigation.
*   **Accessibility Label:** The text VoiceOver reads for a view.
*   **Accessibility Trait:** Semantic role info (button, header, selected).
*   **Element Grouping:** Combining views into one accessible element.
